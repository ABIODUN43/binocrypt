## 3. Cohort Definitions & Verification

### A. Depressed-State Cohort Definition
Strictly causal operational criterion evaluated at bar $t$:
$$\text{Depressed State} \iff (\text{Drawdown}_{90d}(t) \le -35\%) \land (\text{RSI}_{14}(t) \le 38.0) \land (\text{Mom}_{30d}(t) < -10\%)$$
- **Features Used:** Strictly past observations $P_{\le t}, \text{High}_{\le t}, \text{Low}_{\le t}$.
- **Overall Universe Frequency:** 14.82% (13,000 bars).

### B. Comparison Cohort Definition
All label-eligible bars that do not satisfy the depressed-state criterion ($~\text{Depressed} \land \text{Label-Eligible}$).
- Both cohorts are drawn from the identical symbol universe, identical date windows, and evaluated on identical forward horizons.

## 9. Statistical Interpretation & Conclusion

1. **Exact Statistical Conclusion:** `NO_SIGNIFICANT_DIFFERENCE`  
   BR-002.1A does not establish a statistically significant positive advantage for the unconditional depressed-state rule. The point estimates are generally unfavorable (-1.25% at 14D, -4.97% at 30D, -1.38% MFE at 14D), but under the cross-sectional Panel Block Bootstrap (5,000 resamples of 10-day calendar blocks), the primary 95% confidence intervals span across zero: **[-4.35%, +1.97%]** for 14D returns and **[-9.88%, +0.35%]** for 30D returns. Because the confidence intervals include zero when market-wide cross-sectional clustering is properly accounted for, the null hypothesis of no mean difference cannot be rejected at $\alpha = 0.05$. Failing to reject $H_0$ is not proof that the underlying distributions are identical, but confirms that this naive rule provides no positive expected edge.

2. **Observed Direction of Point Estimates (Adverse Drift):**  
   While statistically indistinguishable from zero under broad cross-sectional variance, the empirical point estimates lean adverse rather than favorable:
   - Average forward returns from unconditional depressed states are negative (-0.15% at 14D, -2.00% at 30D).
   - In depressed states, downside stops are hit before upside targets with heavy frequency: Barrier A (+25% before -10%) is stopped out in **70.22%** of cases (vs 65.11% in comparison states); Barrier B (+50% before -20%) is stopped out in **66.37%** of cases.

3. **Core Research Implication & Revised Gate for BR-002.1B:**  
   - **Baseline Finding:** The naive strategy ("down -35% + RSI low = buy") is definitively disproven as an edge. Buying simply because an asset is deeply depressed is largely catching falling knives.
   - **Revised Prerequisite for BR-002.1B:** The lack of an unconditional advantage in BR-002.1A does **not** block BR-002.1B. Rather, it serves as the foundational baseline justifying it. Inside the 13,000 depressed observations, the population is a mixture of continued deteriorations (~70% falling knives) and a minority of genuine accumulation inflections (~10–20%).
   - **The BR-002.1B Research Question:** *Among observations that are already in the depressed state, can causal features $X_t$ available at time $t$ (exhaustion, swing structure, higher lows, volume absorption, relative strength) distinguish observations with favorable future upside/risk outcomes from those that continue failing?*
